FAQ

Is it safe to use an online password generator?

It is safe when the generator runs entirely in your browser, like this one. PassForge uses your browser's built-in crypto.getRandomValues() and makes zero network requests — you can load the page, go offline, and generate passwords with the internet unplugged.

Password or passphrase — which should I use?

For accounts where you paste from a password manager, a 16+ character random password is ideal. For things you must type or remember (computer login, master password), a 4–6 word passphrase like "ember-walrus-cargo-tulip" is far easier to remember at comparable strength.

How long should a password be in 2026?

At least 16 random characters for important accounts, or a passphrase of 4+ random words. Below 12 random characters, modern GPU rigs can brute-force many hashes uncomfortably fast.

How is the crack time estimated?

We compute entropy bits from your settings (length × log₂ of the character pool, or words × log₂ of the word list), then assume an offline attacker testing 10 billion guesses per second and report the average time to hit your password.

What does "exclude look-alikes" do?

It removes characters that are easy to confuse when reading or typing a password by hand: 0/O/o, 1/l/I/|, and quote-like marks. Slightly less entropy per character, far fewer typing mistakes.

Are the generated passwords stored anywhere?

No. They exist only on your screen and in your clipboard when you copy one. There is no server, no logging, no analytics on the generated values.
